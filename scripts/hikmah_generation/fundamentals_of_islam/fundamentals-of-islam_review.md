## Lesson 1: The Foundations of Islamic Belief: God's Existence and Oneness

**Summary:** This lesson establishes the rational and theological foundations of Islamic belief in God's existence and absolute oneness (Tawhid). Drawing on the principle of cause and effect, it demonstrates through philosophical reasoning — supported by Qur'anic verses and narrations from Imam Ali (a.s.), Imam al-Baqir (a.s.), Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.), and Imam al-Ridha (a.s.) — that a self-existing, non-composite Being must underlie all of creation. The lesson then explores Tawhid's logical implications, ruling out polytheism, trinity, incarnation, and anthropomorphism, before presenting a structured framework of God's affirmative and negative attributes. Finally, it situates Tawhid as the first and animating principle of the five fundamental articles of Twelver Shia faith.

**Estimated Time:** 18 minutes

**Prerequisites:**
- Familiarity with basic logical reasoning is helpful, as the lesson uses philosophical arguments — such as cause and effect and the impossibility of infinite regress — to build its case for God's existence.
- A basic understanding of the Twelver Shia tradition and its key figures, including the Prophet Muhammad (s.a.w.) and the twelve Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, will help the reader appreciate the theological sources cited throughout.
- Some awareness of classical Islamic sources such as Nahj al-Balagha and al-Kafi will help the reader contextualise the narrations and sermons quoted extensively in this lesson.

**Key Terms:**
- **Tawhid**: The Islamic doctrine of the absolute, unqualified oneness of God — affirming that Allah is unique, indivisible, and without any partner, equal, or internal distinction.
- **Ahl al-Bayt**: Literally 'People of the House'; refers to the Prophet Muhammad's household, and in Twelver Shia Islam specifically to the Prophet, Fatimah al-Zahra, and the twelve Imams.
- **Nahj al-Balagha**: A celebrated collection of sermons, letters, and sayings attributed to Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (a.s.), compiled by the scholar al-Sharif al-Radi in the 10th–11th century CE.
- **Kalam**: Islamic scholastic theology; the discipline that uses rational and philosophical argumentation to explore and defend Islamic doctrinal positions.
- **'Adalah**: Divine Justice; the Twelver Shia article of faith affirming that Allah is perfectly just and never acts unjustly toward His creation.

### Page 1: The Principle of Cause and Effect: A Rational Path to God

## The Principle of Cause and Effect: A Rational Path to God

One of the most foundational questions any thinking person can ask is: why does anything exist at all? Islamic theology addresses this question through a rigorous philosophical framework rooted in the principle of cause and effect.

Every thing or being whose existence depends on something else is called an **effect**. The thing upon which that existence depends is called a **cause**. This relationship of dependence is universal — everything we observe in the material world owes its existence to something prior to it.

### The Divine Refusal to Bypass Causality

Remarkably, the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt taught that Allah Himself honours this principle of causality as a universal law governing creation. Imam Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Sadiq (a.s.) — the sixth Imam of the Twelver Shia — is reported to have said:

***"Allah did not want to let things work except through their means and reasons. For everything He made a cause, for every cause an explanation, for every explanation a science, for every science a speaking chapter. Some people know it and some do not know. It is the messengers of Allah and we."***

*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, narrated by Rib'i ibn 'Abdallah from Abu 'Abdallah (a.s.), Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

This profound narration affirms that the causal order is not a constraint upon God but a reflection of His wisdom — a structured, knowable system whose deepest meanings are entrusted to the Prophet and the Imams.

### The Impossibility of an Infinite Regress

The critical insight of causal reasoning is that the chain of causes and effects cannot regress infinitely into nothing. An effect cannot owe its existence to non-existence. If we trace any chain of dependent beings back far enough, reason compels us to conclude that there must be a **self-existing cause** — a being that does not depend on anything else for its existence. The absurdity of a universe built upon nothingness is self-evident.

The Qur'an poses this challenge directly to the human intellect:

> أَمْ خُلِقُوا مِنْ غَيْرِ شَيْءٍ أَمْ هُمُ الْخَالِقُونَ

*"Or were they created by nothing? Or are they themselves the creators?"* (52:35)

> أَمْ خَلَقُوا السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بَلْ لَا يُوقِنُونَ

*"Or created they the heavens and the earth? Nay, they have no certainty."* (52:36)

These verses invite humanity to reflect rationally. The two impossible alternatives — that the universe created itself from nothing, or that human beings are their own creators — expose the logical necessity of an external, self-subsisting Creator.

### The Nature of the Ultimate Cause

Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (a.s.) — also known as Abu Turab, the Commander of the Faithful and the first Imam — articulated the metaphysical nature of this self-existing Being in his celebrated sermon on Divine Unity (*Tawhid*):

***"Everything that is known through itself has been created, and everything that exists by virtue of other things is the effect (of a cause)... His Being precedes times. His Existence precedes non-existence and His eternity precedes beginning."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on Tawhid, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

This is a precise philosophical statement: any being that can be known or defined through its own composition is, by that very fact, a created thing — an effect. Only the Being whose existence is entirely self-sufficient and uncaused can serve as the ultimate ground of reality.

This understanding aligns with Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i's analysis in *al-Mizan*, where he explains that the Creator, as the Essential Being, is the ultimate Cause of all that exists in the universe. The relationship between Allah and the universe — in its entirety and in each of its components — is that of cause and effect. Real existence flows from the Real Cause, and every effect traces its chain of causality back to Him.

*(al-Mizan, Vol. 5, Surah Al 'Imran, Philosophical Discourse on Attribution of Kingdom)*

### Types of Causes

Classical Islamic philosophy distinguishes between different types of causes to sharpen this reasoning:

- **Structural causes** are those that make up the internal composition of a thing, including the *material cause* (what something is made of) and the *formal cause* (the shape or arrangement that makes it what it is).
- **Creative or agential causes** are the originating factors that bring a structure into existence without being part of the structure itself. These include the *active cause* (the initiating agent) and the *final cause* (the purpose or goal of the thing's existence).

Every composite being — that is, any being made up of parts — is necessarily dependent on those parts for its existence. This makes it an *effect*, not a self-sustaining reality. Since every material, dimensional being occupies space and is therefore geometrically divisible, no material being can serve as the ultimate self-existing foundation of reality.

Imam Ali (a.s.) states this with precision:

***"He is One, but not by counting. He is everlasting without any limit. He is existent without any support."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on the Attributes of Allah, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

### Beyond the Material Realm

This line of reasoning forces us entirely beyond the material world, in search of a non-composite, non-dimensional, self-existing Being upon which the entire chain of cause and effect ultimately rests. Allamah al-Tabataba'i reinforces this point by noting that the universe — with all its creatures and interrelated components — forms a single interconnected entity, all of whose links ultimately lead back to the one ultimate Cause.

It is this Being — uncaused, non-composite, unlimited by time or space — that Islamic theology identifies as Allah, the one God, the Creator of all that exists.

### Page 2: The Absolute Oneness of God: Tawhid and Its Implications

## The Absolute Oneness of God: Tawhid and Its Implications

Having established through reason that a self-existing, non-composite Being must underlie all of creation, Islamic theology proceeds to describe the nature of this Being. The central doctrine is **Tawhid** — the absolute, unqualified Oneness of God.

---

### Why God Must Be One

If we were to propose the existence of two self-existing beings, we would immediately encounter a logical difficulty. Two truly distinct beings must differ from each other in some respect; otherwise they would be identical and therefore one. But if they differ, each possesses something the other lacks — meaning each is *limited* and therefore *composite*, dependent on the combination of what they share and what distinguishes them. A composite being, as established previously, cannot be self-existing. Therefore, there can only be **one** truly self-existing Being, unlimited and undivided in any sense.

This Being transcends all description in terms of space, time, genus, or differentia. It is beyond every form of dimensional and non-dimensional limitation.

This rational conclusion is powerfully expressed in the famous opening of Imam Ali's (عليه السلام) first sermon in *Nahj al-Balagha*, compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi:

*****"The foremost in religion is the acknowledgement of Him, the perfection of acknowledging Him is to testify Him, the perfection of testifying Him is to believe in His Oneness, the perfection of believing in His Oneness is to regard Him Pure, and the perfection of His purity is to deny Him attributes… whoever attaches attributes to Allah recognises His like, and whoever recognises His like regards Him two; and whoever regards Him as two recognises parts for Him; and whoever recognises parts for Him mistook Him."*****

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon 1, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

### Self-Consciousness of the Divine

Because the Absolute Being has no parts spatially separate from one another, it is never absent from itself. This complete presence-to-itself is what we mean by **consciousness**. Dimensional beings, composed of parts occupying different regions of space, are in a sense absent from their own parts — lacking true self-consciousness in the deepest sense.

The Absolute Being, being wholly undivided and present to itself, is therefore fully self-conscious and consequently conscious of all that exists through it. This is why Islamic tradition refers to God with the personal pronoun **"He"** (هو, *Huwa*) — not as a gender marker, but as acknowledgment of a real, conscious, self-aware Person at the foundation of all existence.

Imam al-Ridha (عليه السلام) — referred to in hadith as *Abu al-Hassan the 2nd* — elaborated on this transcendence in a narration preserved in *al-Kafi*:

*****"How can a person speak of One from Whose perceiving all the senses are frustrated and even the imagination is not able to comprehend Him… He is far above the description of those who speak of His attributes and the praise of those who speak of His praise. He is far in His nearness and is near in His being far… No one can say about Him 'where' because He is far above the conditions and places."*****

*(Al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Kitab al-Tawhid, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

---

### What Tawhid Rules Out

The doctrine of absolute oneness carries profound consequences for how God is understood:

- **No polytheism**: Multiple gods would imply limitation and dependence, contradicting absolute self-existence.
- **No trinity or dualism**: Any internal division in the divine nature implies composition and dependence.
- **No incarnation**: The infinite cannot be fully contained within the finite. As Allamah Tabataba'i explains in *Al-Mizan*, those who claim that God became man — such as certain Christian doctrines — ultimately affirm the existence of God but attach to Him the attributes of defect and transience. *(Tafsir Al-Mizan, Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:116–120, Vol. 11, Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i)*
- **No anthropomorphism**: God cannot be described by physical attributes such as body, color, or location.

Imam Muhammad al-Baqir (عليه السلام) — known as *Abu Ja'far* — firmly rejected any division of God's attributes into separate faculties, as recorded in al-Kafi:

*****"He is One and only One without any multiplicity in different meanings… Allah, the Most High, indeed is above all things. He is All-hearing and All-seeing. He hears with what He sees, and sees with what He hears… Allah is High Exalted above all such things. Only such things are perceived which have the attributes of created things. Allah is not created."*****

*(Al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Kitab al-Tawhid, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

As the Qur'an declares:

> وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

*"Nothing is the like of Him."* (112:4)

> لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

*"Nothing is like of Him, and He is the Hearer, the Seer."* (42:11)

---

### Tawhid as the Pulse of All Islamic Life

Allamah Tabataba'i reminds us in *Al-Mizan* that true Tawhid is not merely a theoretical conviction but must permeate all of life's actions:

*****"Islam has no general objective except the belief in one God in both stages of faith and action together… monotheism permeates in its esoterics and exoterics, and the pure servitude is manifested from his words, deeds and all aspects of his existence."*****

*(Tafsir Al-Mizan, Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:116–120, Vol. 11–12, Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i)*

This vision of Tawhid as the animating principle of all human conduct is precisely why it stands at the head of the Twelver Shia articles of faith.

---

### The Five Fundamental Articles of Faith

According to the Twelver Shia school (*Ithna Ashari*), the fundamental articles of faith are five, each flowing logically from the others:

1. **Tawhid** — Belief in the absolute oneness of God
2. **'Adalah** — Belief in divine justice
3. **Nubuwwah** — Belief in prophethood
4. **Imamah** — Belief in divinely appointed leadership
5. **Qiyamah** — Belief in resurrection and the final purpose of creation

Together, these articles form a coherent and integrated worldview, rooted in the reality of the one, conscious, self-existing God — a God Whom no description can contain, no imagination can reach, and Whom no creature can ever fully encompass.

### Page 3: The Attributes of God: Affirmative and Negative

## The Attributes of God: Affirmative and Negative

While the Divine Essence ultimately transcends the full grasp of human intellect, Islamic theology — and particularly the rich tradition of Twelver Shia *kalam* (scholastic theology) — provides a coherent framework for speaking meaningfully about God. His attributes are organized into two broad categories: **affirmative (Thubutiyyah)** attributes and **negative (Salbiyyah)** attributes.

---

### Negative Attributes

Negative attributes define God by what He is *not* — they systematically negate all forms of limitation, composition, and creaturely characteristics from His essence. For example:

- He is not a physical body with color, shape, or size.
- He cannot be seen, imagined, or fully conceived by any creature.
- He is not localized in space or bounded by time.
- He is not comparable to or countable among created beings.
- Nothing co-exists alongside Him as an extension or emanation of His being.

All such negations reduce to a single fundamental principle: the negation of limitation and composition from the Divine Essence.

This is powerfully expressed by Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (peace be upon him) in *Nahj al-Balagha*, the collection compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi:

> ***"Imagination cannot surmise Him within the limits of movements, limbs or senses. It cannot be said about Him: 'whence?'; and no time limit can be attributed to Him by saying 'till'. He is apparent, but it cannot be said 'from what'. He is hidden, but it cannot be said 'in what'. He is not a body which can die, nor is He veiled so as to be enclosed therein."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on the Attributes of Allah — "الخالق جل وعلا", al-Sharif al-Radi)*

Furthermore, the Imam articulates the logical consequence of attempting to describe God through creaturely categories:

> ***"He who describes Him limits Him. He who limits Him numbers Him. He who numbers Him rejects His eternity. He who said 'how' sought a description for Him. He who said 'where' bounded Him."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon "في صفات الله جل جلاله وصفات أئمة الدين", al-Sharif al-Radi)*

Allamah Tabataba'i, in his tafsir *Al-Mizan* (Vol. 1, Surah Al-Baqarah), similarly warns against two extremes: those who project material qualities onto God, and those who reduce all divine attributes to mere negatives without positive content. True theological understanding lies between these two pitfalls.

---

### Affirmative Attributes: Three Categories

The affirmative attributes of God are organized into three tiers, each flowing naturally from the previous.

#### 1. Essential Attributes (*Sifat al-Dhat*)

These are qualities implied by the very concept of absolute divine unity, identical with God's essence and not defined in relation to anything outside Him. They include:

- *Oneness* — He is utterly unique and undivided
- *Self-existence* — He depends on nothing else
- *Self-consciousness* — He is fully present to Himself
- *Self-love* — The perfection of His self-awareness
- *Life* — The totality of all the above

In each of these attributes, the subject and object are identical. There is no distinction between the Knower, the knowing, and the known.

Imam Ali (peace be upon him) expresses this incomparable Oneness by contrasting it with every other claimant to unity:

> ***"Everyone called one (alone) save Him is by virtue of being small (in number); and everyone enjoying honour other than Him is humble. Every powerful person other than Him is weak. Every master (owner) other than Him is slave (owned). Every knower other than Him is seeker of knowledge."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon "وفيها مباحث لطيفة من العلم الالهي", al-Sharif al-Radi)*

#### 2. Essential Relative Attributes (*Sifat al-Haqiqiyyah al-Izafiyyah*)

These describe God in relation to beings other than Himself:

- *Knowledge of other entities* — He knows all things, visible or unseen, material or spiritual
- *Will* — His love for expression and manifestation
- *Love of other beings* — His grace extended toward creation
- *Might* — His total authority over all that exists

Hearing and Seeing also belong to this category, signifying that all objects of perception are wholly present before God — not through any physical faculty, but through His infinite encompassing awareness.

Imam Ali (peace be upon him) describes God's all-encompassing knowledge:

> ***"He is the Hearer but not by means of any physical organ. He is the Looker but not by a stretching of eyelids... His knowledge about those who died in the past is the same as His knowledge about the remaining survivors, and His knowledge about whatever there is in the high skies is like His knowledge of whatever there is in the low earth."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon "في صفات الله جل جلاله وصفات أئمة الدين" and Sermon "الخالق جل وعلا", al-Sharif al-Radi)*

As the Qur'an itself affirms:

> وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

***"And perfect has become the word of thy Lord in truth and justice; There is none to change His words, and He is the All-Hearing and the All-Knowing."*** *(Surah Al-An'am, 6:115)*

#### 3. Relative Attributes (*Sifat al-Izafiyyah*)

These are the countless names and descriptions drawn from God's actions and relationships with creation — Creator, Sustainer, Forgiver, Ruler, Helper, and innumerable others. Their full number is known only to God:

> قُلِ ادْعُوا اللَّهَ أوِ ادْعُوا الرَّحْمَنَ أَيَّا مَا تَدْعُوا فَلَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ

***"Call Him Allah or ar-Rahman — whether call Him this or that makes no difference; behold that He has innumerable excellent names."*** *(Surah Al-Isra', 17:110)*

Imam Ali (peace be upon him) beautifully captures the paradox of these relational attributes — that God possesses them even prior to any creation:

> ***"He is the Knower even though there be nothing to be known. He is the Sustainer even though there be nothing to be sustained. He is the Powerful even though there be nothing to be overpowered."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon "في صفات الله جل جلاله وصفات أئمة الدين", al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

### Summary

These three tiers of attributes form a coherent and ascending hierarchy: the relative attributes flow from the essential relative ones, which in turn flow from the purely essential attributes. All of them point back to the single, infinite, incomprehensible Divine Essence — the *Allah* whose reality surpasses every human concept, yet whose existence is the most certain of all realities.

As Imam Ali (peace be upon him) summarizes in one of the most celebrated passages of *Nahj al-Balagha*:

> ***"Praise be to Allah who is well-known without being seen, Who creates without pondering over, Who has ever been existent when there was no sky with domes, nor curtains with lofty doors, nor gloomy night, nor peaceful ocean."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon "وتشتمل على قدم الخالق وعظم مخلوقاته", al-Sharif al-Radi)*

## Lesson 2: 'Adalah and Nubuwwah: Divine Justice and the Necessity of Prophethood

**Summary:** This lesson explores two core articles of faith in Twelver Shia Islam: 'Adalah (divine justice) and Nubuwwah (prophethood). Divine justice is presented not as a legal abstraction but as a metaphysical expression of God's perfection, inseparable from His grace, manifesting in the precise and proportionate ordering of all creation. Nubuwwah is established as a logical necessity rooted in God's role as Rabb (Cherisher), who must provide humanity—His vicegerent on earth—with authoritative guidance through prophets. The lesson concludes by explaining why prophethood ends with the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ), whose everlasting miracle, the Qur'an, continues as the definitive proof of divine guidance, carried forward through the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt.

**Estimated Time:** 16 minutes

**Prerequisites:**
- Familiarize yourself with the Five Roots of Religion (Usul al-Din) in Twelver Shia Islam—Tawhid (divine oneness), 'Adalah (divine justice), Nubuwwah (prophethood), Imamah (leadership), and Ma'ad (resurrection)—as this lesson focuses on the second and third of these foundational beliefs.
- Understand the basic Islamic concept of Tawhid (the oneness of God) and God's absolute perfection, since the lesson builds its arguments about divine justice and prophethood directly from God's nature and attributes.
- Have a general awareness of the concept of prophethood in the Abrahamic traditions—that God sends messengers to guide humanity—as the lesson explores why this is a rational necessity rather than merely a historical fact.

**Key Terms:**
- **'Adalah**: Divine justice; in Twelver Shia theology, the belief that God always acts in perfect accordance with His own nature and purpose, placing everything precisely where it belongs—making injustice metaphysically impossible for God.
- **Nubuwwah**: Prophethood; the belief that God sends divinely appointed prophets equipped with miracles to guide humanity, understood in Shia theology as a logical requirement of God's role as the Cherisher (Rabb) of creation.
- **Rububiyyah**: Divine lordship and cherishing; the quality by which God not only creates but continuously nurtures and guides every being toward the fulfilment of its potential.
- **Khalifah**: Vicegerent or representative; used in the Qur'an to describe humanity's unique role as God's representative on earth, entrusted with reflecting divine attributes and upholding justice.
- **Mu'jizah**: A prophetic miracle; a supernatural act that serves as verifiable evidence of a prophet's divine appointment, beyond the ordinary capacity of human beings.

### Page 1: Divine Justice ('Adalah): God's Action as Pure Grace and Precision

## Divine Justice ('Adalah): God's Action as Pure Grace and Precision

The second fundamental article of faith in the Twelver Shia tradition is **'Adalah** — divine justice. This is not merely a legal concept but a profound metaphysical truth about the nature of God's action in the universe.

---

### Justice as an Expression of God's Inner Life

To understand divine justice, we must trace it back to its source in God's essence. God's self-consciousness gives rise to self-love. Self-love gives rise to a desire for self-expression — what we might call the **will to act**. Since God is absolute and possesses all the means for expression within Himself, His will to act manifests as *creation*. Creation is therefore the expression of God's love and might, not a response to any external need.

Because God's action flows from His absolute perfection, it is necessarily in complete harmony with its own purpose. This perfect synchronization of action with purpose — placing everything in its precise position to create unity and achieve the intended goal — *is what we mean by divine justice*.

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i articulates this beautifully in his monumental tafsir:

*"**He must be proceeding on the straight path of nature, and should not deviate from it — neither to the right nor to the left. In short, he should put every thing in its right place... The system found in the universe — and it is the handiwork of Allāh — is pure justice.**"*
*(Al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, Surah Al 'Imran, Vol. 5, Allamah Tabataba'i)*

Justice and action are therefore **inseparable** in God. He could not act unjustly without contradicting His own nature.

---

### Grace and Justice as Two Faces of One Reality

God's creative act, being the expression of pure love, is an act of **grace** — freely given, with no expectation of return. This grace pervades all of creation. Yet not every creature receives the same measure of grace. Each being receives divine grace in proportion to its capacity and merit. This precise, proportionate distribution — nothing more and nothing less than what the recipient can hold — is **justice**.

Grace implies justice: true generosity gives what is truly appropriate. Justice implies grace: truly fair treatment is an act of love. Neither can be fully understood without the other. Together, they are expressed in one of the most frequently used divine titles in the Qur'an: **Rabb al-'Alamin** — the Lord Cherisher of all the worlds.

This inseparability of love and justice is beautifully captured in a narration from Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (عليه السلام) — the sixth Imam of the Twelver Shia tradition — who said:

*"**Justice is sweeter than water is for a thirsty man. How vast is justice when it is practiced in an issue even though it would be very little!**"*
*(Al-Kafi, Vol. 2, Chapter on Justice, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni; narrated via Ali ibn Ibrahim from his father, from Ibn Abi 'Umayr, from Hammad, from al-Halabi)*

The same Imam's practical expression of this principle is equally striking. It is narrated that when a pack animal in his own caravan was unevenly loaded, the Imam immediately instructed:

*"**O boy, adjust the load on this animal, for Allah the Exalted loves justice.**"*
*(Man La Yahduruh al-Faqih, Vol. 2, Shaykh al-Saduq; narrated by Ibn Faddal from Hammad al-Lahham)*

Divine justice, in other words, is not an abstract theological principle — it descends into the smallest acts of everyday life.

The Qur'an connects divine justice directly to the purpose of prophethood:

> لَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا رُسُلَنَا بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ وَأَنْزَلْنَا مَعَهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْمِيزَانَ لِيَقُومَ النَّاسُ بِالْقِسْطِ

*"Indeed, sent We Our apostles with clear proofs, and sent We down with them the Book and the Scale that people may establish themselves in justice."* (57:25)

---

### Justice as the Goal of Human Life

Just as justice is the supreme quality of God's action, so too is it the ultimate goal of human life and civilization. The Qur'an presents justice as the standard by which all human virtues and vices are measured — both in this world and on the Day of Judgment. Allamah Tabataba'i further notes that divine reward and punishment are not based on imaginary or merely conventional considerations, but on realities that actually exist within the system of creation:

*"**The merit and demerit of deeds are based on things which actually exist and have a system... Allāh brings every thing to the perfections of its existence, to the final goal of its being.**"*
*(Al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, Surah Al 'Imran, Vol. 6, Allamah Tabataba'i)*

Every person carries the record of their own deeds:

> وَكُلَّ إِنْسَانِ اَلْزَمْنَاهُ طَائِرَهُ فِي عُنُقِهِ وَنُخْرِجُ لَهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كِتَابًا يَلْقَاهُ مَنْشُورًا

*"We have tied the actions of every man to his neck; which we will bring forth unto him on the day of resurrection in the form of an unfolded book."* (17:13)

> اقْرَأْ كِتَابَكَ كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ الْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا

*"Read thou thy book, Sufficeth thine own self today, as reckoner against thee."* (17:14)

This places immense moral weight on every individual action. This individual moral accountability extends also to those who hold positions of authority. Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (عليه السلام) — also known as Abu Turab, the Commander of the Faithful, and the first Imam of the Twelver Shia — is reported to have said:

*"**The hand of Allah hovers with mercy above the head of the judge. But if he deviates in judgment, Allah the Mighty and Majestic leaves him to himself.**"*
*(Man La Yahduruh al-Faqih, Vol. 3, Bab al-Hayf fi al-Hukm, Shaykh al-Saduq; narrated by al-Sakuni with his chain of transmission)*

The moral logic is consistent: God's justice supports those who embody justice, and withdraws its special protection from those who abandon it.

All of this is not cold, impersonal accounting — it is the expression of a God whose justice is inseparable from love, and whose standard is always perfectly calibrated to what each soul truly deserves.

### Page 2: Nubuwwah: The Necessity of Prophethood and Divine Guidance

## Nubuwwah: The Necessity of Prophethood and Divine Guidance

The third fundamental article of faith in Twelver Shia Islam is **Nubuwwah** — belief in prophethood. This is not merely a historical claim about certain individuals from the past. It is a logical necessity rooted in the very nature of God as the *Cherisher* (Rabb) of all creation.

---

### Creation Requires Guidance

God does not simply create and then abandon His creation. The concept of *Rububiyyah* (divine cherishing and lordship) means that God provides each created thing with all the means it requires to develop its full potential. The Qur'an expresses this principle beautifully:

> قَالَ رَبُّنَا الَّذِي أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلْقَهُ ثُمَّ هَدَىٰ

*"Said he (Musa): 'Our Lord is He Who gave unto everything its (suitable) form, and then (also) guided it (aright).'"* (Surah Ta-Ha, 20:50)

This principle operates at every level of creation — from the formation of stars and minerals, to the growth of plant life and animals, and ultimately to the emergence of **man**: the most complex and comprehensive being in the universe, uniquely gifted with intellect, free will, and the capacity for both discovery and innovation.

---

### Man as the Vicegerent of God

The creation of man marked a decisive turning point in the unfolding of creation. Unlike all other beings, man was fashioned with the potential to consciously embody and reflect God's attributes — to serve as a comprehensive mirror of divine excellence. This is precisely what the Qur'an refers to when it speaks of God's **vicegerent (khalifah)** on earth:

> وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً

*"Recollect (O' Our Apostle Muhammad!) When said thy Lord unto the angels: 'Verily I (intend to) appoint a vicegerent on earth.'"* (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:30)

This representative status is confirmed in the story of Adam being taught the names and demonstrating that knowledge before the angels — a knowledge the angels themselves did not possess. The first human being carried within him the *potential* for all excellences, and the Qur'an affirms:

> لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنْسَانَ فِي أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍ

*"Indeed, We created man in the best structure (mould)."* (Surah At-Tin, 95:4)

---

### Why Prophets Are Necessary

Given that man is endowed with free will and can fall short of his potential, the principle of divine cherishing (*Rububiyyah*) logically demands that God furnish clear, authoritative guidance. A person claiming to be a divine teacher and representative must provide **verifiable evidence** of that claim — something beyond the ordinary capacity of human beings. This is the concept of a **miracle** (*mu'jizah*): the seal of divine appointment.

The Qur'an documents the unbroken chain of prophets sent by God throughout history:

> إِنَّا أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْكَ كَمَا أَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ نُوحِ وَالنَّبِيِّينَ مِنْ بَعْدِهِ وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَالْأَسْبَاطِ وَعِيسَىٰ وَأَيُّوبَ وَيُونُسَ وَهَارُونَ وَسُلَيْمَانَ

*"Verily, We have revealed unto thee (O' Our Apostle Muhammad) as we did reveal unto Noah and the Apostles after him; We did reveal unto Abraham and Ismael and Isaac and Jesus and Job and Jonah, and Aaron, and Solomon."* (Surah An-Nisa', 4:163)

From the Shia perspective, this chain of prophethood is not arbitrary — each prophet carried with him a recognition of the divine authority that would ultimately culminate in the mission of the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) and be continued through his Ahl al-Bayt (his blessed household). Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (عليه السلام) is reported to have said:

***"There has never been a prophet who came except with the knowledge of our rights and our preeminence over others."***

*(Reported by 'Abd al-A'la from Imam Abu 'Abdillah [al-Sadiq], transmitted via Yunus b. Ya'qub — al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

This narration reveals a profound theological point: every prophet, from Adam to Jesus ('Isa), was aware of the special station of the Ahl al-Bayt of the final Prophet. Prophethood and the subsequent guidance of the Imams are thus inseparable dimensions of a single divine plan.

---

### The Prophet Muhammad and the Completion of Guidance

Imam Ali (عليه السلام) — also known as Abu Turab, a title of honour given to him by the Prophet — eloquently described the mission of the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) in *Nahj al-Balagha*:

***"Allah deputed the Prophet with a sparkling light, a clear argument, an open path and a guiding book... Through him Allah disclosed the ways that had been forsaken, and destroyed the innovations that had been introduced. Through him He explained the detailed commands."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on the description of the Prophet and his Ahl al-Bayt, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

The same source describes the Qur'an — the Prophet's greatest miracle and the enduring evidence of his prophethood — as:

***"A light whose flames cannot be extinguished, a lamp whose gleam does not die, a sea whose depth cannot be sounded, a way whose direction does not mislead... a mine of belief and its centre, the source of knowledge and its oceans."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on Allah's Omniscience, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

### Conclusion

Prophethood is not an arbitrary divine act — it is God's structured and necessary response to the human need for guidance. It is the clearest expression of His justice and grace working in harmony, to lead humanity toward the fulfilment of its highest potential as His vicegerent on earth. Every prophet was a light sent to illuminate one chapter of this ongoing divine guidance, a chain that — in the Twelver Shia understanding — finds its perfection in the Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) and continues through the divinely appointed Imams of his household.

### Page 3: The Finality of Prophethood and the Everlasting Miracle of the Qur'an

## The Finality of Prophethood and the Everlasting Miracle of the Qur'an

### The Law of Divine Selection

The chain of prophethood was not random or arbitrary. From Adam onward, God maintained a special lineage through which the potential for perfect submission and leadership was preserved and transmitted. This principle is known as the **Divine Law of Selection (Istifa')**:

> إِنَّ اللَّهَ اصْطَفَىٰ آدَمَ وَنُوحًا وَآلَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَآلَ عِمْرَانَ عَلَى الْعَالَمِينَ

*"Verily, God did choose Adam and Noah and the descendants of Abraham and the descendants of Imran above all the worlds."* (3:33)

This selection is not merely a matter of hereditary privilege. It reflects a divinely protected natural disposition (*Ismat al-Khalqi*) toward perfect receptivity of divine guidance — a disposition that forms the foundation for the *infallibility of will* these chosen individuals demonstrate upon reaching full consciousness.

---

### Why Prophethood Has an End

Prophethood advanced through history from particular and limited missions toward universal comprehensiveness. The analogy of formal education is instructive: just as a student progresses from primary school through university — after which no new formal schooling is required, though learning itself continues — so too did humanity's guided instruction reach its culmination in the final prophet.

The conclusion of prophethood does not signal stagnation. It means that divine guidance has now been given in its most complete form, and humanity's task is to engage with it rationally and to implement it faithfully. The Qur'an itself was declared complete:

> الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِي وَرَضِيتُ لَكُمُ الْإِسْلَامَ دِينا

*"This day have I perfected for you, your religion, and have completed My favour on you, and chosen for you Islam (to be) the Religion."* (5:3)

A hadith from Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.) — transmitted through Shaykh al-Kulayni in *al-Kafi* — illuminates why the Prophet Muhammad (S), though the last to be *sent*, was the foremost in spiritual station. When certain members of Quraysh asked how he could excel all the prophets while being sent as the seal and final one among them, the Prophet replied:

***"I was the first to believe in my Lord, and the first to answer Allah when He called the prophets to make a covenant and bear testimony against their own souls — when they were asked 'Am I not your Lord?' — I was the first prophet to say: 'Yes, You are our Lord.' I thus excelled them in the acknowledgment of the Oneness of Allah."***

*(Narrated from Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (a.s.), reported via Salih ibn Sahl; al-Kafi, Shaykh Muhammad ibn Ya'qub al-Kulayni, Vol. 1 & 2)*

This narration beautifully explains that the finality of prophethood in time does not imply inferiority of rank. On the contrary, the Prophet's primordial precedence in affirming divine Oneness is precisely what made him the seal of all prophets.

---

### The Qur'an as Everlasting Proof

Among all the evidences offered by the Holy Prophet Muhammad (S) for his mission, the most enduring and comprehensive remains the **Qur'an itself**. Unlike miracles tied to a specific time and place — such as the staff of Moses (a.s.) or the healing miracles of Jesus (a.s.) — the Qur'an stands as an open, accessible, and perpetually unchallengeable demonstration of divine origin.

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i, in his monumental *al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an*, explains that the Qur'anic challenge (*tahhaddi*) was not limited to its eloquence alone — for that would address only Arabic speakers. Rather, the challenge extends to all of humanity across all times:

***"Say: 'If men and jinn should combine together to bring the like of this Qur'an, they could not bring the like of it, even though some of them were aiders of the others.'"*** *(Surah al-Isra', 17:88, cited in al-Mizan, Surah al-Baqarah, Vol. 1, Allamah Tabataba'i)*

The broader challenge — *"produce a chapter like it"* — aims to establish that this Book is a divine, supernatural sign. Once that is established, the prophethood of the Prophet follows necessarily.

The Qur'an itself declares its own awesome gravity:

> لَوْ أَنْزَلْنَا هَذَا الْقُرْآنَ عَلَىٰ جَبَلٍ لَرَأَيْتَهُ خَاشِعًا مُتَصَدِّعًا مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ

*"Had We sent down this Qur'an, on the mountain, thou wouldst certainly have seen it (the mountain) humbled itself and rent asunder for the fear of God."* (59:21)

Imam Ali (a.s.) — also known as *Abu Turab*, a beloved title given to him by the Prophet — speaks of the Qur'an with profound eloquence in *Nahj al-Balagha* (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi):

***"Know that this Qur'an is an adviser who never deceives, a leader who never misleads and a narrator who never speaks a lie. No one will sit beside this Qur'an but that when he rises he will achieve one addition or one diminution — addition in his guidance or elimination in his (spiritual) blindness. Know that no one will need anything after (guidance from) the Qur'an and no one will be free from want before (guidance from) the Qur'an."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on the Excellence of the Qur'an and Warning Against Innovation, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

In a separate sermon, Imam Ali (a.s.) further declares:

***"The Qur'an orders as well as refrains, remains silent and also speaks. It is the proof of Allah before His creation. He has taken from them a pledge (to act) upon it... He let the Prophet leave this world when he had conveyed to the people all His commands of guidance through the Qur'an."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon on the Power of Allah and the Greatness of the Qur'an, al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

### The Prophet: The Perfect Word of God in Creation

The Qur'an is identified in Islamic teaching as the *Muhaymin* — the authoritative guardian over all previous divine scriptures — and as the most perfect expression of God's Word in the realm of legislation. The Prophet Muhammad (S), in turn, is the most perfect word of God in the realm of creation: one who does not speak from personal desire but only from divine revelation:

> وَمَا يَنْطِقُ عَنِ الْهَوَىٰ

> إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا وَحْيٌ يُوحَىٰ

*"Nor he speaketh of (his own) inclination; It (the wording) is naught, but a revelation revealed (unto him)."* (53:3–4)

The completion of prophethood in the person of Muhammad (S) marks not an era of stagnation, but one of rational engagement, implementation, and continued guidance — entrusted to those he appointed as his rightful successors, the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (a.s.).

### Page 1: The Concept of Imamah: Continuity of Divine Guidance

## The Concept of Imamah: Continuity of Divine Guidance

The fourth fundamental article of faith in Twelver Shia Islam is **Imamah** — the belief in divinely appointed leadership as a permanent institution within human society, from the time of Adam until the Day of Resurrection.

---

### Why Imamah Is a Logical Necessity

The principle of divine cherishing (*Rububiyyah*) does not conclude with the sending of prophets. God's care for creation is continuous, and so too must be His provision for human guidance. Just as no engineer constructs a complex system without ensuring its ongoing maintenance, a God who is the Lord-Cherisher of all worlds necessarily ensures that creation is never left without a spiritual pivot.

This pivot — the *Imam* — serves two distinct functions:

1. **As a medium between God and creation**: In this capacity, the Imam receives divine blessings and channels them to the human community. This function must be continuous and uninterrupted, even when the Imam is not publicly visible or accessible.

2. **As a teacher and model for humanity**: In this capacity, the Imam provides direct guidance and instruction. This function operates periodically — whenever humanity requires a new phase of teaching or correction — and need not be perpetually active in its outward form.

The first function means there can never be a complete vacuum in divine leadership. The second function is closely associated with the specific role of **Nubuwwah (prophethood)**. This is why every prophet is also an Imam, but not every Imam must be a prophet in the technical sense. Allamah Tabataba'i elaborates on this distinction in *al-Mizan*, Volume 2, under Surah Al-Baqarah (2:124), explaining that while guidance by Allah's command is essential to the Imam, the reverse — that every divinely guided individual must be an Imam — does not necessarily follow.

---

### The Number and Nature of the Imams

The Twelver Shia tradition holds that the Imams are twelve in total, all from the household (*Ahl al-Bayt*) of the Prophet Muhammad (S). Imam Muhammad al-Baqir (a.s.) is reported in *al-Kafi* to have affirmed this clearly. The companion Zurara narrated:

*"I heard Abu Ja'far say: 'There will be twelve Imams from the family of Muhammad, and they all will be **Muhaddathun** — persons to whom angels speak. They will be of the descendants of the Messenger of Allah and Ali ibn Abi Talib. The Messenger of Allah and Ali are their ancestors.'"*
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Kitab al-Hujjah, Shaykh al-Kulayni)*

(*Muhaddathun* refers to those who receive communications from angels without being prophets themselves — a distinction that underscores the Imams' unique spiritual station.)

In another narration from the same collection, Imam al-Baqir is reported to have said:

*"We are twelve Imams; al-Hassan and al-Husayn are of them, and the rest of the Imams are from the descendants of al-Husayn."*
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Kitab al-Hujjah, Shaykh al-Kulayni)*

Abu Basir also narrated from Imam al-Baqir (a.s.):

*"After al-Husayn ibn Ali there will be nine Imams. The ninth of them will be al-Qa'im (the one who will rise with Divine Authority)."*
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Kitab al-Hujjah, Shaykh al-Kulayni)*

(*al-Qa'im* — "the one who rises" — is a title of the Twelfth Imam, Imam al-Mahdi (a.j.), who is in occultation and will return to establish justice on earth.)

---

### The Relationship Between Prophethood and Imamah

With the completion of prophethood in the person of Muhammad (S), the conveyance of new divine revelation came to an end. However, the role of *preserving, interpreting, and embodying* that revelation continued unbroken. The eleven Imams who succeeded the Holy Prophet over approximately three centuries were not recipients of new scripture, but were divinely gifted with the wisdom to explain and implement the Qur'an and prophetic teachings with complete fidelity.

The Qur'an references the continuation of divine gifts to the family of Ibrahim:

> أَمْ يَحْسُدُونَ النَّاسَ عَلَىٰ مَا آتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ فَقَدْ آتَيْنَا آلَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَآتَيْنَاهُمْ مُلْكًا عَظِيمًا

*"But indeed, We have given to Abraham's children the Book and the Wisdom and We gave them a Great Kingdom."* (4:54)

The three gifts — the Book, the Wisdom, and the Great Kingdom — correspond respectively to the Qur'an, the divinely guided interpretation by the Imams, and the eventual complete establishment of divine authority on earth. Allamah Tabataba'i, in *al-Mizan* (Vol. 10, Tafsir of Surah Al-Ma'idah), draws a parallel between the Imams and the divines (*rabbaniyyun*) who guarded revealed scripture among the Children of Israel — noting that just as the Torah required guardians and witnesses from among the Imams of that nation, so too does the Qur'an require the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt.

The completion of this divinely ordained leadership structure is also connected to the perfection of religion. Allamah Tabataba'i notes in *al-Mizan* (Vol. 9, Tafsir of Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:3) that the verse *"This day have I perfected for you your religion"* is inseparably tied to the appointment of a successor who would carry the *wilayah* (divinely sanctioned authority) after the Prophet — making the institution of Imamah integral to the completion of Islam itself.

---

### Recognising the Imam

A question naturally arises: how does the community identify the rightful Imam after one passes away? Ibn Abi Nasr narrated that he posed this question to Imam Ali ibn Musa al-Ridha (a.s.), the eighth Imam, who replied:

*"For the Imam there are certain signs. Of such signs, one is that he must be the eldest son of his father. Excellence and the will must be found in him... The Armament with us is like the Ark among the Israelites. Imamah is always with the Armament wherever it may go."*
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Kitab al-Hujjah, Shaykh al-Kulayni)*

The "Armament" (*silah*) refers to the sacred relics and weapons of the Prophet, transmitted through the Imams as a symbol of legitimate succession — much as the Ark of the Covenant was a sign of divine authority among the Children of Israel.

---

### The Quality of *Ismat*: Divinely Granted Impeccability

Because the Imam functions as the foremost representative of God's will among humanity, his position demands absolute purity. He must be free from all physical and spiritual defects that could compromise his total submission to God. This quality is called ***Ismat*** — infallibility or impeccability. It is not a human achievement, but a divine gift bestowed upon those whom God selects for this role.

The Imam of every age is understood as the individual most foremost in obedience to God's will and most comprehensive in representing that will to humanity — embodying the very purpose for which, according to the Qur'an, *jinn and mankind were created*.

### Page 2: The Twelve Imams: Their Chain and Significance

## The Twelve Imams: Their Chain and Significance

### The Prophetic Declaration

The Holy Prophet Muhammad (S) made explicit and unambiguous declarations about the continuation of leadership after him. Among the most well-known is the following:

> مَنْ مَاتَ وَلَمْ يَعْرِفْ إِمَامَ زَمَانِهِ مَاتَ مِينَةَ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ

*"Whoever dies without knowing the imam of his time, his death will be paganic death (Jahiliyyah)."*

The Prophet also explicitly declared that the number of his successors would be exactly twelve — no more and no less — and according to the Ithna Ash'ari (Twelver Shia) school, he named each of them individually. This is corroborated by narrations found in both Sunni and Shia hadith collections.

The significance of obedience to these Imams is rooted in the Qur'anic injunction found in Surah An-Nisa:

***"O you who believe! obey Allah and the Messenger and those vested with authority from among you..."***
*(Surah An-Nisa, 4:59)*

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn at-Tabataba'i, in his celebrated *al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an* (Vol. 8, Surah An-Nisa, 4:59–70), documents multiple narrations from the Imams of the Ahlul Bayt confirming that "those vested with authority" (*Uli al-Amr*) refers specifically to the Imams. Jabir al-Ju'fi is reported to have asked Imam al-Baqir (a.s.) about this verse, and the Imam replied: *"The Imams."* Similarly, Ibn Shahrashub narrates that Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.) replied: *"The Imams from the Ahlul Bayt of the Messenger of Allah."*

The chain of Imamah runs from:
1. **Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib ('a)** — the Prophet's cousin and son-in-law
2. Through a succession of divinely guided leaders from the family of the Prophet
3. Down to the eleventh Imam, **Al-Hasan al-Askari ('a)** — also known as Abu Muhammad al-Askari — who passed away on the 8th of Rabi' al-Awwal, 260 AH in Samarra, Iraq

---

### The Twelfth Imam: Al-Mahdi

The twelfth and final Imam is **Al-Mahdi ('a)**, born on the 15th of Sha'ban, 254 AH in Samarra. His birth, much like that of the Prophet Moses for comparable reasons, was kept secret from the Abbasid authorities who sought to eliminate this blessed lineage. Nevertheless, the eleventh Imam regularly introduced his son to trusted and reliable disciples as his rightful successor.

The *al-Kafi* of Shaykh al-Kulayni (Vol. 1) records several such testimonies. In one narration:

***"Ali ibn Muhammad has narrated from Hamdan al-Qalanisi who said: I asked al-'Amri, 'Has Abu Muhammad passed away?' He said, 'Yes, he has passed away, but he has left behind among you a person whose neck is like this' — and he made a sign with his hand (indicating good health and strength)."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

In another narration from the same collection:

***"Ali ibn Muhammad has narrated... that Abu Muhammad (al-Askari) showed him (the Twelfth Imam) to him."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

And again:

***"Muhammad ibn Yahya has narrated... from Abu Nasr Zarif, the servant (of Abu Muhammad), that he saw him (the Twelfth Imam)."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

These narrations collectively confirm that the birth and existence of the Twelfth Imam was known and witnessed, not a matter of speculation.

The Twelfth Imam is described as uniquely uniting in himself the full spiritual and bloodline heritage from Adam, encompassing both the Ishmaelite branch through his father and the Israelite branch through his mother, Lady Nargis (a.s.), who descended from Simon Peter.

---

### The Two Periods of Occultation

Following the passing of the eleventh Imam, the Twelfth Imam entered a state of **occultation (ghaybah)** — present in the world but not publicly identifiable. This state was not unexpected; the Imams had foretold it generations in advance. Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.) is reported to have said:

***"If an occultation reaches you all regarding the Master of this command, then do not reject it."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 1, narrated by Muhammad ibn Muslim from Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.), Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

The occultation unfolds across two distinct phases:

**Minor Occultation (Ghaybat al-Sughra)**: Lasting approximately 70 years (260–329 AH), during which communication with the Imam was maintained through four successive appointed deputies (*Nawwab al-Arba'ah* — the Four Gates):
1. Uthman ibn Sa'id al-Amri
2. Muhammad ibn Uthman
3. Abul Qasim al-Husayn ibn Ruh al-Nawbakhti
4. Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Samarri

**Major Occultation (Ghaybat al-Kubra)**: Beginning with the passing of the fourth deputy in 329/330 AH, this continues until the Imam's eventual reappearance (*Zuhur*). During this era, no single individual is personally appointed as a direct deputy. Instead, qualified scholars (*mujtahids*) — those who are pious, learned in jurisprudence, and capable of deriving legal rulings through sound methodology (*Ijtihad*) — serve as the Imam's general representatives in guiding the community.

The Prophet himself addressed the spiritual benefit believers would draw from the Imam even during occultation. As reported in *Tafsir al-Burhan* and cited by Allamah Tabataba'i in *al-Mizan* (Vol. 8, Surah An-Nisa):

***"Certainly, by Him Who has sent me with prophethood! they will be guided by him during his occultation as people benefit from the sun when it is hidden in cloud."***
*(Prophet Muhammad (S), cited in al-Mizan fi Tafsir al-Qur'an, Vol. 8, Surah An-Nisa, Allamah Tabataba'i)*

This profound analogy reminds us that the Imam's occultation does not diminish his role as a spiritual intermediary between God and creation. Divine grace continues to flow through him, even when his person is hidden from ordinary sight. His occultation is itself a manifestation of divine wisdom — granting humanity the opportunity to deepen its rational and ethical engagement with the completed divine constitution revealed through the Prophet (S).

### Page 3: The Return of the Imam and the Establishment of Divine Justice

## The Return of the Imam and the Establishment of Divine Justice

### The Awaited Reappearance

The occultation of the Twelfth Imam is not a permanent state. According to both Qur'anic indication and numerous prophetic traditions, Imam Al-Mahdi ('a) will reappear when the period of rational development and implementation (*Ijtihad*) reaches its culmination — whether in a right or wrong direction.

His reappearance will coincide with the descent of Prophet Jesus ('a), who will follow and support his mission. Together, they will establish what the Qur'an refers to as the **Great Kingdom (Mulk al-'Adhim)** of the family of Ibrahim — the full and practical execution of divine authority across the entire world.

This fulfills the divine promise:

> وَعْدَ اللَّهِ لَا يُخْلِفُ اللَّهُ وَعْدَهُ وَلَكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ النَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

*"(This is) the promise of God! Faileth not God His promise, but most people know not (this)."* (30:6)

The faithful are encouraged to maintain sincere expectation (*intizār*) of the Imam's reappearance — indeed, the Imams themselves taught that this awaiting is itself a form of spiritual relief. Imam al-Sādiq ('a) is reported to have said:

***"O Abu Basir, are you after worldly gains? In fact, one who has established faith in this matter (the rise of al-Mahdi with Divine Authority) has already experienced the relief of expecting his coming."***
*(Al-Kāfī, Vol. 1, Shaykh al-Kulaynī)*

Furthermore, the Imams warned against those who attempt to assign a fixed date or timeline to the reappearance. Imam al-Sādiq ('a) declared:

***"Those who give it a definite time have lied, the expediting ones perish, and salvation is for those who are submissive."***
*(Al-Kāfī, Vol. 1, Shaykh al-Kulaynī)*

---

### The Minor Resurrection (Raja'ah)

The establishment of the Imam's global kingdom will be accompanied by what Islamic teaching calls the **Minor Resurrection (Raja'ah)** — or the *lesser return*. This refers to a partial gathering of prominent righteous personalities who advanced the sacred cause of divine guidance, alongside notable adversaries who worked against it.

This is emphatically **not** reincarnation or transmigration of souls. Rather, it is a partial gathering — analogous to, but distinct from, the Major Resurrection — in which figures from different eras meet and interact. The distinction between the two resurrections is one of scope: the Raja'ah is partial, while the Major Resurrection is universal and total.

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i offers a profound clarification in his *Al-Mīzān*:

***"The traditions which prove ar-raj'ah, in spite of their large number, are one in their essence. They show that the world is proceeding towards a day when the signs of Allah will appear in their perfect manifestation... At that time some dead persons — some friends of Allah and some of His enemies — shall be returned to the world again, and the truth will be separated from the falsehood."***
*(Al-Mīzān, Vol. 3, Surah al-Baqarah, Allamah al-Tabataba'i)*

The Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt taught that **"the days of Allah are three: the Day of Reappearance, the Day of Return, and the Day of Resurrection."** These three days are one in essential nature but differ in degree and scope — which is why a single Qur'anic verse may, in different narrations, be interpreted as referring to all three.

---

### Important Clarifications About the Imam's Return

It is essential to understand that the reappearance of the Twelfth Imam will **not** alter, abrogate, or add to the religion of Islam as completed through the Prophet Muhammad (S) and explained by his eleven successors. There will be no new revelation and no new divine law. Rather, his era represents the complete and comprehensive *practical implementation* of everything already contained in the Qur'an and prophetic Sunnah — the full blossoming of what was always latent in the divine constitution.

The Imam's extraordinary capacities at reappearance will be spiritual and psychic forces of an order surpassing all known physical power — just as atomic energy surpasses the primitive force of draft animals, so too will the Imam's spiritual authority surpass current human capabilities, removing not only spatial but also temporal barriers.

The Qur'an confirms that God's righteous servants will ultimately inherit the earth:

> وَلَقَدْ كَتَبْنَا فِي الزَّبُورِ مِنْ بَعْدِ الذِّكْرِ أَنَّ الْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحُونَ

*"And indeed, We did write in the Psalms (Zabur, given to David) after the reminder (Taurah, given to Moses), that the earth shall inherit it My righteous servants."* (21:105)

The believers are also reminded that recognizing and maintaining connection to the living Imam carries immense spiritual weight. Imam al-Bāqir ('a) — the fifth Imam — is reported to have said:

***"One who dies knowing who his Imam is — the coming of this matter earlier or later will not affect him at all. One who dies knowing who his Imam is will be like the one present with al-Qā'im in his tents."***
*(Al-Kāfī, Vol. 1, Shaykh al-Kulaynī)*

(*Al-Qā'im*, meaning "the one who rises," is a well-known title of Imam al-Mahdi ('a).)

The entire doctrine of Imamah — from Adam to the Last Imam — is thus the story of God's unbroken commitment to never leave humanity without guidance. This story will reach its glorious conclusion in the age of universal justice.

## Lesson 4: Qiyamah: Resurrection, Judgment, and the Life Hereafter

**Summary:** This lesson covers Qiyamah, the fifth fundamental article of faith in Twelver Shia Islam, exploring death as a transition to heightened consciousness rather than annihilation, the intermediate realm of Barzakh where souls dwell between individual death and collective resurrection, and the detailed stages of the Major Resurrection including judgment, the weighing of deeds, and intercession. Key themes include the reality of the afterlife, the role of the Ahl al-Bayt as witnesses and intercessors, and the ultimate sorting of souls into Paradise or Hell based on faith, sincerity, and deeds.

**Estimated Time:** 20 minutes

**Prerequisites:**
- Twelver Shia Islam recognizes five fundamental articles of faith (Usul al-Din): Tawhid (Divine Unity), Adl (Divine Justice), Nubuwwah (Prophethood), Imamah (Leadership of the twelve Imams), and Qiyamah (Resurrection) — familiarity with these foundations helps contextualize why Qiyamah is given such theological weight.
- The Ahl al-Bayt refers to the Prophet Muhammad's purified household, specifically Sayyidah Fatimah al-Zahra, Imam Ali, and the eleven subsequent Imams; in Shia theology they hold a special spiritual authority as guides, witnesses, and intercessors for the community.
- Islamic theology distinguishes between the individual soul (nafs) and the physical body, holding that the soul is the true self and continues to exist after the body's death — understanding this body-soul distinction is essential for grasping the concepts of Barzakh and resurrection.

**Key Terms:**
- **Barzakh**: The intermediate realm or 'barrier' between an individual's death and the collective Day of Resurrection, where the soul continues to exist consciously and begins to experience the consequences of its earthly deeds.
- **Shafa'ah**: Intercession — the divinely authorized act by which prophets, the infallible Imams, and Sayyidah Fatimah al-Zahra plead before God on behalf of souls on the Day of Judgment; only intercession explicitly permitted by God is valid.
- **Sirat**: The Bridge stretched over Hell that all souls must cross on the Day of Judgment; its crossing is described as thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword, and safe passage depends on one's fulfillment of trusts, prayer, and monotheism.
- **Qiyamah al-Kubra**: The Major Resurrection — the final, collective rising of all creation (humans, jinn, and other beings) to stand before God for ultimate judgment, as distinguished from the individual transition of death.
- **Tawfiq**: Divine enablement or grace — the God-given capacity and opportunity to do good; often invoked in supplications asking Allah to grant one the ability to act righteously.

### Page 1: The Nature of Death and the Intermediate Stage

## The Nature of Death and the Intermediate Stage

The fifth and final fundamental article of faith in Twelver Shia Islam is **Qiyamah** — belief in resurrection and the ultimate judgment of all creation. The Arabic word *Qiyamah* literally means "to rise," and as a religious concept it signifies *rising to a higher state of existence*.

The Holy Prophet Muhammad (S) expressed this succinctly:

***مَنْ مَاتَ فَقَدْ قَامَتْ قِيَامَتُهُ***

***"He who dies, his rise has been realised."***

### What Death Actually Is

Contrary to common human anxiety, death in the Islamic worldview is not annihilation or extinction. From the Qur'an and the teachings of the Ahl al-Bayt (the Prophet's household and the twelve Imams), several foundational truths about death are established:

1. **The conscious self continues**: After separation from the material body, the ego remains conscious of itself and its past.

2. **Death is a migration, not destruction**: It is a passage from a stage of lesser sensitivity to one of higher sensitivity — a refinement, not an ending.

3. **Senses are sharpened, not destroyed**: All sensory and cognitive faculties become more refined and acute after death.

4. **The material connection is severed**: What ends at death is only the direct link between the conscious self and the outer material world through the external senses.

5. **Attention turns inward and upward**: The soul's focus shifts to its stored memories, its inner dimensions, and the higher spiritual realms above it.

This understanding is powerfully supported by the teaching of Imam Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Sadiq (a.s.) — the sixth Imam — who described what happens to the soul immediately after its departure from the body:

***"When the soul is taken, it hovers above the body, whether the person is a believer or otherwise, watching everything that is done to it. When the body is shrouded and placed on the bier and carried on the shoulders of men, the soul returns to it and enters it, and its sight is extended to see its place in Paradise or Hellfire. It then calls out with a loud voice: If it is from the people of Paradise, it says, 'Hasten me, hasten me!' And if it is from the people of Hellfire, it says, 'Take me back, take me back!' It is aware of everything done to it and hears the words spoken."***

*(Reported from Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.), Shaykh al-Saduq, Vol. 1)*

This hadith confirms that consciousness is not only preserved at death but is actually expanded — the soul perceives its ultimate destination with a clarity impossible during earthly life.

The Qur'an also speaks to this sharpening of perception at the moment of death:

> لَقَدْ كُنْتَ فِي غَفْلَةٍ مِنْ هَذَا فَكَشَفْنَا عَنْكَ غِطَاءَكَ فَبَصَرُكَ الْيَوْمَ حَدِيدٌ

***"(It will be said): Thou wert heedless of this, now have We removed from thee thy veil, so thy sight today is sharp."*** *(Surah Qaf, 50:22)*

This is further confirmed by the Prophet's tradition:

***النَّاسُ نِيَامٌ فَإِذَا مَا تُوا انْتَبَهُو***

***"Man is (at present) in a state of deep sleep. When he dies, he will be awake."***

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i, in his monumental *Tafsir al-Mizan* (Vol. 13, commentary on Surah Al-An'am, 6:60), draws a profound analogy between sleep and death in explaining the Qur'anic verse: *"And He it is Who takes your souls at night (in sleep)..."* He explains that just as sleep involves a partial withdrawal of the soul from the body, death involves its complete withdrawal — and the term *tawaffa* (taking the soul in full) confirms that the soul is the complete essence of the human being, not merely one of its components.

### The Intermediate Stage (Barzakh)

Between the death of an individual and the collective resurrection of all creation lies the **intermediate stage (*Barzakh*)**. This is a genuine realm of existence — not a metaphor or symbolic state — where the departed soul begins to experience the fruits of its individual faith and deeds.

> فَلَوْلَا إِذَا بَلَغَتِ الْحُلْقُومَ

***"Why then do ye not (help) when the soul of the dying man cometh up to the throat?"*** *(Surah Al-Waqi'ah, 56:83)*

> وَمِنْ وَرَائِهِمْ بَرْزَخٌ إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَنُونَ

***"And after them shall be a barrier until the day when they are raised (again)."*** *(Surah Al-Mu'minun, 23:100)*

Allamah al-Tabataba'i, in *Tafsir al-Mizan* (Vol. 2, commentary on Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:154), affirms that the life of *al-Barzakh* is a real, not imaginary, existence. He notes that while the Resurrection on the Day of Judgment is unambiguously described throughout the Qur'an, the Barzakh life has been described in a manner that has led some to overlook it — yet the evidence firmly establishes its reality.

The nature of souls dwelling in the Barzakh is described vividly in multiple narrations from Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.):

***"The souls are in the form of bodies in a tree of Paradise; they converse and recognise one another. When a soul joins them, they say, 'Leave it, for it has escaped a great terror.' Then they ask it, 'What did such-and-such person do, and what did such-and-such person do?' If it responds, 'I left them alive,' they have hope for them. But if it responds, 'They have died,' they say, 'They have fallen.'"***

*(Imam al-Sadiq (a.s.), narrated by Abu Basir; Shaykh al-Saduq, Vol. 1; also recorded in al-Kafi, Shaykh al-Kulayni, Vol. 3, Chapter on the Soul after Death)*

This remarkable hadith reveals several dimensions of the Barzakh:

- **Souls retain their personal identity** and bodily form, even in the spiritual realm.
- **They maintain awareness of the living** and concern for those they have left behind.
- **The transition through death is itself an ordeal** — those already in the Barzakh understand this and extend compassion to the newly arrived soul.
- **The fate of those who die without faith is one of loss and descent** (*hawa* — falling into the deep).

A complementary narration from Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (a.s.) — also known as *Amir al-Mu'minin*, the Commander of the Faithful — recorded in al-Kafi (Vol. 3, Shaykh al-Kulayni) further establishes that the souls of unbelievers dwell in a place of suffering: ***"The worst well in the fire is the well of Barahut, in which the souls of unbelievers live."***

In the Barzakh, the soul is confronted with its individual record of deeds and questioned by the angelic guardians appointed to it. It cannot act to redeem past wrongs at this stage. However — and this is profoundly significant in Shia theology — the prayers and righteous acts of living believers can bring some relief to souls in the Barzakh. More profoundly still, the intercession of the prophets and the twelve Imams — whose spiritual bond with the community of faith transcends physical death — can bring genuine aid to souls dwelling in this intermediate realm.

There is no rebirth, no return to the lower stage of earthly consciousness, and no transmigration of souls. The direction of existence is always upward, always toward God.

### Page 2: The Major Resurrection: Stages of Judgment

## The Major Resurrection: Stages of Judgment

### The Two Blowings of the Trumpet

The **Major Resurrection (Qiyamah al-Kubra)** is the final collective rising of all beings — humans, jinn, and other creatures — to face the ultimate manifestation of God's authority:

> يَوْمَ يَقُومُ النَّاسُ لِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

*"The day when mankind shall stand before the Lord of the worlds."* (83:6)

This momentous event is set in motion by two distinct blowings of a trumpet by the angel Israfeel. Allamah Tabataba'i, in his commentary on Surah Al-An'am (6:73), illuminates the significance of this trumpet: the blowing gathers all of creation together just as a trumpet summons soldiers for a critical moment — a universal marshalling for the final reckoning before the One whose kingdom is absolute on that Day.

***"And He it is Who has created the heavens and the earth with truth; and on the day He says: 'Be', it is. His word is the truth, and His is the Kingdom on the day when the trumpet shall be blown; the Knower of the unseen and the seen; and He is the Wise, the Aware."*** (Surah Al-An'am, 6:73 — *al-Mizan*, Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn at-Tabataba'i, Vol. 13)

The **first blowing** produces a cosmic severance — all bonds of kinship, lineage, and social identity dissolve. Every being loses the form it had in temporal life:

> فَإِذَا نُفِخَ فِي الصُّورِ فَلَا أَنْسَابَ بَيْنَهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ وَلَا يَتَسَاءَلُونَ

*"And when the trumpet is blown, there shall be no ties of kindred between them on that day, nor shall they ask one another."* (23:101)

The **second blowing** restores all beings into a new and heightened form of existence. God has made known that certain souls — the foremost among the foremost, those who stand in the highest cognitive and spiritual station — will not be overcome by the swoon of the first blowing, by His will.

---

### Multiple Substages of the Resurrection

The Day of Resurrection is not a single instant but an expansive sequence of interconnected stages. Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (peace be upon him), in his famous *al-Khutbah al-Gharra'* (the Brilliant Sermon), vividly described this gathering:

***"Till when matters come to a close, the world dies and resurrection draws near. Allah would take them out from the corners of the graves, the nests of birds, the dens of beasts and the centres of death. They hasten towards His command and run towards the place fixed for their final return — group by group, quiet, standing and arrayed in rows. They will be within Allah's sight and will hear every one who would call them..."***
*(Nahj al-Balagha, al-Khutbah al-Gharra', Sermon 83 — al-Sharif al-Radi)*

This vivid description aligns with the detailed hadith narrated from Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn (Zayn al-Abidin, peace be upon him), who transmitted on the authority of Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib (peace be upon him):

***"When it will be the Day of Judgement, Allah will resurrect the people from their graves in their isolation, without beards, in one plane, being driven by the light, and gather them in the darkness until they are paused upon the hurdle on the Place of Resurrection... And this will be the first sensation from the sensations of the Day of Judgement."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 8 — Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni; narrated via Suwayr ibn Abi Fakhta from Imam Zayn al-Abidin, from his father al-Husayn, from Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib)*

The substages of this immense Day include:

**1. Summoning by One's Imam (Leader)**

Each person is called forth in association with the leader they followed in this life:

> يَوْمَ نَدْعُو كُلَّ أُنَاسٍ بِإِمَامِهِمْ

*"Remember the day of judgment when We will summon every people in the name of their imam (leader)."* (17:71)

The same hadith from al-Kafi clarifies the Shia understanding of this verse: the Imams (peace be upon them) are present as witnesses on the Day of Resurrection, each testifying over the people of his era.

***"The registers of deeds would have been published, the scales set, and the Prophets and the witnesses — and these would be the Imams — would be present. Each Imam testifying over the people of his era, having stood among them by the Command of Allah Mighty and Majestic, and called them to the Way of Allah."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 8 — Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

**2. Presentation of the Book of Deeds**

Every individual and community receives the complete and unaltered record of their actions for review.

**3. The Witnessing**

From within each community, a witness from among themselves stands. The Holy Prophet Muhammad (ṣallallahu 'alayhi wa alihi wa sallam) stands as the supreme witness over all:

> وَيَوْمَ نَبْعَثُ فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ شَهِيدًا عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِهِمْ وَجِتُّنَا بِكَ شَهِيدًا عَلَىٰ هَؤُلَاءٍ

*"And on the day when We will raise up in every people from among themselves a witness upon them and bring thee (O Our Apostle Muhammad!) as a witness upon all these."* (16:89)

**4. The Balancing of Deeds**

Every deed, no matter how minute, is weighed with absolute precision and complete justice:

> وَنَضَعُ الْمَوَازِينَ الْقِسْطَ لِيَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ فَلَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْئًا وَإِنْ كَانَ مِنْقَالَ حَبَّةٍ مِنْ خَرْدَلٍ أَتَيْنَا بِهَا

*"And We shall set up the balances of justice on the day of judgment, so no soul shall be dealt with unjustly in the least; and even if there be the weight of a grain of mustard seed, We will bring it into account."* (21:47)

The hadith of al-Kafi further describes the divine proclamation on this Day:

***"I am Allah. There is no god except for Me. I am the Equitable Judge Who will not let any injustice be done today. I shall judge between you all with justice and equity. No one will be done injustice in My presence today..."***
*(al-Kafi, Vol. 8 — Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

**5. The Stage of Reckoning**

Each soul is made accountable for itself, with its own record as evidence — yet Allah and His authorized agents oversee the entire process. The hadith from Imam Zayn al-Abidin also addresses the mechanics of settling wrongs between individuals: good deeds may be transferred from the oppressor to the oppressed, and sins from the oppressed may be added to the oppressor if no good deeds remain.

**6. The Stage of Intercession**

Those whom Allah has authorized to intercede — the prophets, the purified Imams (peace be upon them), and Sayyidah Fatimah al-Zahra' ('a) — present their intercession on behalf of those under their spiritual guardianship.

**7. The Final Grouping**

All beings are sorted into their ultimate categories and led to their permanent abode. As Imam Ali (peace be upon him) described in Nahj al-Balagha:

***"He would then divide them into two groups, rewarding one and punishing the other. As regards the obedient people, He would reward them with His nearness and keep them forever in His house... As for people of sins, He would settle them in the worst place..."***
*(Nahj al-Balagha — al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

The stages of the Major Resurrection remind every believer that this life is a period of preparation, and that accountability before the Lord of the Worlds is an absolute certainty. May Allah grant us tawfiq to stand on that Day with the righteous, summoned in the company of the purified Imams (peace be upon them).

### Page 3: Intercession, Paradise, and Hell: The Final Destination

## Intercession, Paradise, and Hell: The Final Destination

### The Doctrine of Intercession (*Shafa'ah*)

One of the most consequential and frequently misunderstood dimensions of Islamic eschatology is the concept of **intercession (*Shafa'ah*)** — the act by which divinely authorized beings will plead on behalf of others before God on the Day of Judgment. The Qur'an both negates certain forms of intercession and affirms others, and grasping this distinction is foundational to a correct understanding of the afterlife.

**Intercession that will not prevail**: Any hope placed in idols, self-invented mediators, or beings who have received no divine authorization is entirely futile. The Qur'an declares:

> أَمِ اتَّخَذُوا مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ شُفَعَاءَ قُلْ أَوَلَوْ كَانُوا لَا يَمْلِكُونَ شَيْئًا وَلَا يَعْقِلُونَ

*"Or have they taken intercessors other than God? Say thou: 'What! Even though they have no power over anything, nor do they understand.'"* (Surah al-Zumar, 39:43)

**Intercession that will prevail**: God, in His wisdom and mercy, has designated specific beings, granted them His permission, and approved their word:

> يَوْمَئِذٍ لَا تَنْفَعُ الشَّفَاعَةُ إِلَّا مَنْ أَذِنَ لَهُ الرَّحْمَنُ وَرَضِيَ لَهُ قَوْلًا

*"On that day shall avail not intercession (of any) save (that of) whom hath permitted God, and whose word He is pleased with."* (Surah Ta-Ha, 20:109)

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i, in his monumental tafsir *al-Mizan* (Vol. 1, commentary on Surah al-Baqarah), explains that the verses apparently negating intercession must be read alongside those affirming it. The Qur'an negates intercession without divine permission, then immediately makes an exception for those whom God has authorized — a literary device used throughout the Qur'an to emphasize the absolute sovereignty of God while still affirming the reality of sanctioned intercession. He further notes that after the people of Paradise have settled in the gardens and the people of Hell in their abode, the intercessors will then intercede for a group of the guilty, rescuing them by God's leave.

The authorized intercessors are the prophets, the infallible Imams (peace be upon them all), and Sayyidah Fatimah al-Zahra ('a) — collectively known as the **Ahl al-Bayt** (the purified household of the Prophet). The Qur'an attests to their unique spiritual station:

> إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنْكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا

*"Verily, verily God intendeth but to keep off from you (every kind of) uncleanliness O' ye the people of the house and purify you (with) a thorough purification."* (Surah al-Ahzab, 33:33)

The Prophet ﷺ himself declared the inseparability of the Qur'an and his blessed progeny in the famous *Hadith al-Thaqalayn*:

> نِي تَارِك فِيكُمُ التَّقْلِينِ كِتَابَ اللهِ وَعِتَ رَبِّي أَهْلَ بَيْنِي مَا إِنْ تَسْتَكْتُمْ بِهِمَا لَنْ تَضِلُوا بَعْدِي ابَدًا

*"I am leaving amongst you two weighty things, one of which is greater than the other. These are, the Book of Almighty Allah and my progeny. So, take care how you treat them after me. The twain shall never part company until they come to me at the Pool [of Kawthar]."*

---

### The Bridge of the Sirat and the Terror of Hell

The vivid reality of the final reckoning is not merely theoretical. Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ('a) — the fifth Imam and a great-grandson of the Prophet ﷺ — transmitted a profound narration from the Prophet ﷺ himself regarding the terrifying approach of Hell and the crossing of the Bridge (*al-Sirat*) on the Day of Judgment:

***"The Trustworthy Spirit (Jibreel) informed me that Allah, there is no god apart from Him, when He gathers the former ones and the later ones, Hell will be brought by a thousand reins, each rein held by one hundred thousand angels from the stern and mighty ones... Then a Bridge would be placed over it, thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword. It would have three hurdles: the first being the fulfilment of trusts and mercy; the second being the Salat; and the third being the Lordship of the Worlds... And the one who is saved would be so by the Mercy of Allah."***

*(Al-Kafi, Vol. 8, narrated from Imam al-Baqir ('a) from the Prophet ﷺ, via Jabir; Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

This narration reinforces the Qur'anic truth that crossing to salvation is not guaranteed by ancestry or assumption, but by faithfulness to trust, prayer, and divine monotheism.

Imam Ali ('a) — also known as *Abu Turab* ("Father of Dust"), a title of honor given to him by the Prophet ﷺ — captured this reality powerfully in a sermon delivered upon receiving the pledge of allegiance in Medina, preserved in *Nahj al-Balagha* (compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi):

***"Beware that sins are like unruly horses on whom their riders have been placed and their reins let loose, so that they jump with them into Hell. Beware that piety is like trained horses on whom the riders have been placed with the reins in their hands, so that they take the riders to Heaven."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon 16; al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

### The Three Final Groups

At the conclusion of the resurrection, all souls are sorted into three ultimate categories, as described in Surah al-Waqi'ah:

1. **The People of the Right Hand** — righteous believers who enter Paradise
2. **The People of the Left Hand** — those who rejected truth and will face divine punishment
3. **The Foremost of the Foremost** — those nearest to God, the intercessors and witnesses who oversee and guide others

Imam Ali ('a) alluded to this tripartite division in the same sermon from *Nahj al-Balagha*:

***"He who has heaven and hell in his view has no other aim. He who acts quickly and strives succeeds, while the seeker who is slow may still entertain hope, and he who falls short of action faces destruction in Hell."***

*(Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon 16; al-Sharif al-Radi)*

---

### What Earns Entry into Paradise?

A narration preserved by Shaykh al-Saduq (Vol. 4) through a chain leading to Imam Muhammad al-Baqir ('a) records the Prophet ﷺ as saying:

***"Whoever's final words are 'La ilaha illa Allah' (There is no God but Allah) will enter Paradise. Whoever's life ends while fasting for a day will enter Paradise. And whoever's final deed is giving charity sincerely for the sake of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, will enter Paradise."***

*(Shaykh al-Saduq, Vol. 4; narrated from Imam al-Baqir ('a) from the Prophet ﷺ)*

This hadith underscores a crucial insight: it is not simply the sum of one's deeds, but the *orientation of one's heart* at the moment of death — toward God in sincerity — that ultimately matters.

---

### Paradise and Hell: Genuine Realities, Not Metaphors

The descriptions of Paradise and Hell in the Qur'an are to be understood as authentic realities, not allegorical constructs — even if expressed in terms our present, sense-bound intellects can only partially grasp. Our current faculties are too constrained to fully conceive of the life to come, just as someone born blind cannot truly appreciate a painting's beauty.

What the Qur'an and the teachings of the Imams make clear is that after death, all human faculties are *refined and immeasurably sharpened*. The pleasures of Paradise will therefore be incomparably richer than anything experienced in this world, and the pain of Hell correspondingly more intense. As the Qur'an reminds us:

> وَمَا هَذِهِ الْحَيَاةُ الدُّنْيَا إِلَّا لَهْوٌ وَلَعِبٌ وَإِنَّ الدَّارَ الْآخِرَةَ لَهِيَ الْحَيَوَانُ لَوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ

*"And nothing is this life of the world but a vain sport and play; and verily the abode of the hereafter is certainly the life; if they but know."* (Surah al-'Ankabut, 29:64)

All of temporal existence — its fleeting pleasures and its passing pains — is, in this light, but a faint shadow of the living realities that await every soul.

### Page 1: The Social Dimension of Faith: Justice, Leadership, and Community

## The Social Dimension of Faith: Justice, Leadership, and Community

One of the most important — and sometimes overlooked — aspects of Islam as presented in the Qur'an and taught by the Ahl al-Bayt (the Prophet's household, peace be upon them) is its fundamentally **social** character. The five principles of faith are not merely abstract metaphysical beliefs. They carry direct, transformative implications for how human beings relate to one another, how communities are organized, and how justice is pursued in the world.

---

### Why Religion Cannot Be Purely Individual

Some religious traditions treat faith primarily as a private bond between the individual soul and God. The Qur'an explicitly rejects this reduction. It presents creation and resurrection as a fundamentally *collective* reality:

> مَا خَلْقُكُمْ وَلَا بَعْتُكُمْ إِلَّا كَنَفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ إِنَّ اللَّهَ سَمِيعٌ بَصِيرٌ

*"Your creation and resurrection are nothing but that of a single soul."* (Surah Luqman, 31:28)

This verse is remarkable in its scope. Human beings are not isolated atoms drifting randomly through time. Their creation is linked, their resurrection is linked, and therefore their moral and spiritual lives are deeply intertwined. The success or failure of one member of a community touches all others in ways both visible and invisible.

Allamah Sayyid Muhammad Husayn al-Tabataba'i, in his monumental tafsir *Al-Mizan*, reflects on this very theme when discussing how the weakening of religion's social role leads to devastating consequences for humanity. He observes that when faith is reduced to private sentiment and stripped of its social and legal dimensions, it inevitably yields ground to materialism and moral collapse — a trajectory he traces through the history of Western religious thought (Al-Mizan, Surah Al-'Imran [3], Vol. 6). This is precisely the pitfall the Qur'anic worldview guards against by insisting on the communal nature of human existence and divine accountability.

---

### The Imam as the Axis of Social Life

This social reality is why **Imamah** — the divinely appointed leadership of the community after the Prophet ﷺ — is not merely a political succession dispute, but a profound theological necessity. The universe, in its totality, resembles a vast congregational prayer: ordered, purposeful, and directed toward God. Just as a congregational prayer requires an Imam to lead it, so too does the human community require a divinely guided Imam at its center — one who receives blessings from God on behalf of all and channels them to all.

The Prophet ﷺ himself drew attention to the centrality of the Imam's role in community life:

***"The Imam of the people is their representative, so choose the best among you to lead."***

*(Arabic: إِمَامُ ٱلْقَوْمِ وَافِدُهُمْ فَقَدَّمُوا أَفْضَلَكُمْ — Shaykh al-Saduq, Vol. 1)*

This hadith highlights that the Imam is not merely a ritual functionary but a representative (*wafid*) of the entire community before God. The concept extends far beyond the prayer hall: the Imam's moral and spiritual uprightness determines the quality of the community's collective orientation toward the Divine.

In prayer, we say *"we"* rather than *"I"* — a practice that encodes this social theology into the daily rhythms of worship. Every believer prays not merely for their own guidance, but as a member of a community journeying toward God together:

> اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ

*"Guide **us** to the straight path"* — the "us" is unmistakably collective.

The standards governing who may lead this communal prayer are therefore stringent. Imam Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Sadiq (peace be upon him) — the sixth Imam of the Twelver Shia — stated clearly:

***"Three people should not be prayed behind: the one who is unknown (al-majhūl), the extremist (al-ghālī) even if he claims to hold your beliefs, and the one who openly commits sinful acts (al-mujāhir bil-fisq) even if he appears moderate."***

*(Arabic: ثَلاَثَةٌ لاَ يُصَلَّى خَلْفَهُمُ ٱلْمَجْهُولُ وَ الْغَالِي وَ إِنْ كَانَ يَقُولُ بِقَوْلِكَ وَ الْمُجَاهِرُ بِالْفِسْقٍ وَ إِنْ كَانَ مُقْتَصِداً — Shaykh al-Saduq, Vol. 1; also narrated in Shaykh al-Tusi, Vol. 3)*

The term *ghālī* (extremist) refers to one who exaggerates in religious claims beyond legitimate bounds, while *mujāhir bil-fisq* denotes one who commits moral transgressions openly and without shame. The fact that such persons are disqualified from leading even the ritual prayer reflects the broader principle: **leadership of the community requires moral and spiritual integrity**, not merely formal affiliation.

---

### Justice as the Organizing Principle of Society

Because God's own action is defined by justice — the precise and loving distribution of what each being deserves — human society must similarly be organized around justice. The Qur'an identifies this as the very purpose of prophetic mission:

> لَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا رُسُلَنَا بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ وَأَنْزَلْنَا مَعَهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْمِيزَانَ لِيَقُومَ النَّاسُ بِالْقِسْطِ

*"Indeed, We sent Our apostles with clear proofs, and sent down with them the Book and the Scale, that people may establish themselves in justice."* (Surah al-Hadid, 57:25)

The "Scale" (*Mizan*) here refers not only to a physical weighing instrument but to a comprehensive standard of balance — the entire system of divine revelation, prophetic teaching, and Imamate — that enables human beings to correctly measure their actions, relationships, and social arrangements. An infallible scale requires an infallible user; this is why the role of the Imam is indispensable to the establishment of true justice in the world.

Allamah al-Tabataba'i addresses a related misconception in *Al-Mizan*, cautioning against the view held by some sociologists that "the reality of religion and its fundamental objective is to establish social justice, and the matters related to divine worship have secondary position." He affirms that while social justice is indeed central to Islam, it cannot be separated from *tawhid* (monotheism) and sincere worship — the two are inseparable (Al-Mizan, Surah Al-'Imran [3], Vol. 7). Justice without its divine foundation degenerates into mere politics; worship without its social implications becomes hollow ritual.

---

### The Interdependence of Qiyamah and Imamah

The doctrine of resurrection (*Qiyamah*) and the doctrine of Imamah are so deeply interrelated that they can scarcely be treated separately. The resurrection is the final, ultimate manifestation of God's justice over all creation. The Imamah is the ongoing, historical expression of that same justice within human society. Both point toward the same goal: the complete realization of divine will across every dimension of existence — personal, social, historical, and cosmic.

The Prophet ﷺ beautifully captured the weight of individual moral agency within this collective framework:

***"A believer alone is a proof (Hujjah), and a believer alone is a congregation."***

*(Arabic: الْمُؤْمِنُ وَحْدَهُ حُجَّةٌ وَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُ وَحْدَهُ جَمَاعَةٌ — Shaykh al-Saduq, Vol. 1)*

This hadith is profound in its implications: even a solitary believer who upholds the truth embodies the entire community (*jama'ah*) in their commitment to God. The social and the individual are not opposites — each true believer carries the weight of the whole within themselves, just as the Imam carries the entire community before God.

### Page 2: Submission, Spiritual Growth, and the Purpose of Human Existence

## Submission, Spiritual Growth, and the Purpose of Human Existence

### What Islam Truly Means

The word *Islam* is derived from the Arabic root for *submission* and *peace*. But this submission is not passive resignation or blind obedience. It is the active, conscious, and love-driven alignment of a created will with the will of the Creator — the process by which a finite being progressively reflects the infinite attributes of God.

The Qur'an describes this as the fundamental purpose of human creation:

> إِنَّ الدِّينَ عِنْدَ اللَّهِ الْإِسْلَامُ

*"Verily the religion with God is Islam (submission to God's will)."* (3:19)

This is not a religion that appeared for the first time in 7th-century Arabia. It is the original, primordial orientation of every human soul — the *fitrah* (innate nature) with which every person is born. The diversity of religious traditions across history is not evidence that God has given different religions to different peoples, but rather the result of human deviation from the one original truth.

### The Nature of True Submission

The Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt (the Prophet's purified household) shed profound light on what genuine submission (*taslim*) actually means. In a narration recorded by Shaykh al-Kulayni, a companion mentioned to Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (A) — also known as Abu 'Abdallah — that a man in their community named Kulayb would hear any teaching from the Imams and immediately say, "I submit to it," earning him the title *Kulayb al-Taslim* ("Kulayb the Submissive"). The Imam expressed kindness toward him and then asked those present a searching question:

***"Do you know what submission is?" We remained silent. The Imam said, "That, by Allah, is al-Ikhbat in the words of Allah, the Most Majestic, the Most Gracious: 'The righteously striving believers who are humble before their Lord.'" (11:23)***

*(Al-Kafi, Vol. 1, Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

The Arabic term *al-ikhbat* (الإخبات) denotes a deep, heartfelt humility and inner stillness before God — not merely verbal agreement or outward compliance. True submission, according to the Imam, is a transformation of the inner self: a state of genuine spiritual lowliness and openness before the divine will. This teaching profoundly enriches our understanding of what it means to call oneself a Muslim.

### The Progressive Purification of the Self

The journey of submission is simultaneously a journey of self-purification and progressive spiritual growth. As a person deepens their knowledge of God, their love for God grows. As their love grows, their will begins to harmonize more fully with God's will. At the highest stages of this journey, the will of the servant becomes a perfect expression of the will of the Master — not by losing one's identity, but by expanding it into the vastness of divine love.

The Qur'an beautifully describes the spiritual station of the Holy Prophet:

> وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ

*"Thou art endowed with (sublime) great character."* (68:4)

This sublime character is the goal toward which every believer strives. The means of striving include not only personal worship and moral refinement, but engagement with the whole community of faith, attachment to the Ahl al-Bayt, and a commitment to establishing justice in the world.

### The Role of Love (*Mawaddah*) in Salvation

One of the most striking aspects of Qur'anic teaching on the path to God is the centrality of **love**. God commands the believers through the voice of the Prophet Muhammad (S):

> ذَٰلِكَ الَّذِي يُبَشِّرُ اللَّهُ عِبَادَهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ قُلْ لَا أَسْأَلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًا إِلَّا الْمَوَدَّةَ فِي الْقُرْبَىٰ

*"Say thou (O' Our Apostle Muhammad!): I demand not of you any recompense for it (the toils of the apostleship) save the love of (my) relatives."* (42:23)

This verse carries layers of meaning that the Imams themselves clarified. Imam Muhammad al-Baqir (A) — *Abu Ja'far*, as he is also known — was asked about the continuation of this very verse: *"Whoever achieves virtue will have its merit increased"* (42:23). He explained:

***"'Achievement' refers to one's submission to us, his speaking the truth from us, and not to ascribe any lies to us."***

*(Al-Kafi, Vol. 1, narrated by Muhammad ibn Muslim from Imam al-Baqir; Shaykh Muhammad b. Ya'qub al-Kulayni)*

This narration reveals that love for the Ahl al-Bayt is not merely sentimental devotion, but an active spiritual practice: it involves submitting to their authority, truthfully conveying their teachings, and protecting their message from distortion. When a person genuinely loves the purified Imams — those whose souls are closest to God in the hierarchy of creation — they begin to orient their own consciousness toward the divine through that love. Their ego expands beyond its narrow individual limits, and through participation in the perfection of their beloved, they draw nearer to the Absolute Perfect One.

This explains why the tradition emphasizes that the love of Muhammad and the family of Muhammad is itself the highest reward in this life and the next. As the Persian poet Sa'di wrote, quoted in the foreword of this work:

> عشق محمد كيس است و آل محمد

*"It is enough to absorb the love of Muhammad and Aali Muhammad."*

The entire edifice of Islamic belief — from the rational proof of God's existence, through divine justice, prophethood, Imamah, and resurrection — ultimately serves this one supreme purpose: drawing the human soul into ever-deeper communion with its Lord, through knowledge, submission, and love.
